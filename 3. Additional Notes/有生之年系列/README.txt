Preface

The author of this series does not want to show the name on the notes.
But one day you will know who is that if you stay in the IMO training for a sufficiently long time.
This series of notes is called 有生之年系列 because once I said that who else could read everything in this series in their 有生之年.
One implication is that there are a lot of materials in the folder.
Another meaning is that the notes will be updated from time to time in my 有生之年 (in MO).


Introduction 懶人包

1. If you are new to the IMO training, you should join all the classes in level 1 training.
2. If you forget some basic things or miss some classes, you should read Basic Notes.
3. If you know some basic things and you want to have more practices, you should work on Elementary Problems.
4. If you are confident with your basic knowledge and you aim at entering the IMO team, you should read Intermediate Notes and work on IMO HK TST and CHKMO.
5. If you expect you will be a team member and aim at better results in the IMO, you should work on the IMO Shortlist.
6. If you are a mature team member, you should know what to do.
7. If you are bored, you can read Advanced Notes and Fun.
Lastly, https://artofproblemsolving.com/community/c13_contests (AoPS) contains many useful materials.
But only use it if you know how to use it. It is not friendly to beginners.

1. Basic Notes

The basic notes were originated from the CGMO training.
So its emphasis is slightly different from the IMO training.
For example, in the algebra part, it covers more on inequalities, and it includes the topic of complex numbers.
Complex numbers are usually regarded as out of syllabus in the IMO, but it is needed in the CMO and CGMO.

There are two sets of basic notes in the folder.

The Very Basic Note series mainly lists out all the fundamental knowledge that you should know in the four areas with some basic examples.
You may refer to this set of notes to go through some definitions and basic results if you forget these.
It is not helpful if you wish to know how to solve some problems.

The Very Standard Tricks series covers some standard and well-known methods of solving some basic MO problems.
It does not mean the examples must be simple, but everyone in this area should learn these.
Very likely all these tricks will be covered in the level 1 training.
So it is mainly for those who would like to do more revision on a particular topic,
say if you miss some classes or you are particularly weak at some topics.


2. Intermediate Notes

The intermediate notes form the core part of this series.
The main feature of this set of notes is that it not only tells you the definitions, results and solutions to contest problems,
but also tells you how to analyze the problems and think of the solutions.
This process of thinking is much more important than the solutions because you can use the same ideas to solve many more other problems.

Although the intermediate notes are mostly self-contained and begin with some extremely basic stuff,
the readers are expected to be well equipped with the basic knowledge before reading these,
since many problems in the notes are of IMO level.

Among the notes, the more important ones are given below.
- Functions
- Inequalities
- Elementary Number Theory
- Pure Geometry with Directed Angles
- Definitions and Facts in Plane Geometry


2.1 Functions

Functional equations is one major topic in algebra in the IMO.
This note covers the techniques in solving problems in functional equations and other problems involving functions,
such as functional inequalities and number theory.
In addition, some harder problems in functions usually require some insight from analysis.
Therefore, this note also covers some knowledge in analysis, which is of undergraduate level.


2.2 Inequalities

The first draft of this note was prepared over 10 years ago.
At that time, inequality problems were classic.
Therefore, this note also contains many classical inequality problems and results,
such as the AM-GM inequality and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, etc.
Homogeneous inequalities are of old style as well.
One still needs to learn these, but such problems are less popular in recent years.

Compared to these, the more useful topics are local refinement, induction and combinatorial inequalities.
The local refinement method is a way of thinking that is indeed useful in many different areas,
though this is mainly used to solve inequality problems.
Induction is the most basic technique needed everywhere in mathematics.
Many simple inequality problems can be solved solely using induction.
Combinatorial inequalities refer to inequality problems which involve some combinatorial ideas.
Those are usually much harder problems.
As I am not good at combinatorics, this note may not give you some good insight in solving such problems.
But at least you should be aware of such methods in solving inequality problems.


2.3 Techniques in Combinatorics

This note is not well written because I am not good at combinatorics as said.
Many solutions in this note are not original.
This means I may not really know how one could think of such solutions,
and I may not know what the key step in the solution is.
There is a lack of combinatorial insight in solving harder problems.
However, it is still useful because it provides a lot of useful techniques in this area for which one should learn.


2.4 Pure Geometry with Directed Angles

This is the core note among all the geometry notes.
It covers all the basic and important stuff in geometry and is suitable for beginners.
We only assume the readers have the most basic knowledge in MO geometry.

There are many useful topics contained in this note,
including the standard usages of angle chasing, similar triangles, concyclicity,
geometric transformations, trianle geometry, power and radical axes.
In the second half of the note, we introduce more tricks and advanced topics in geometry,
which are occasionally found to be useful.

The note also introduces the use of directed angles.
It is only a convenient tool that one may use when there is a configuration issue.
It is not helpful for you to solve geometry problems.
So it is not necessary for one to learn this.
In particular, do not learn directed angles unless you are confident that you are a careful person.

Due to my limited ability in this area,
the solutions to many hard problems in this note are not original,
including a number of solutions given by the old trainees.
Therefore, the ideas and the insight explained in the analysis before the problems may not be too accurate.

Strong candidates who aim at solving hard geometry problems are recommended to look for their own ways to solve geometry problems,
including trigonometry, inversion, coordinate geometry, projective geometry, etc.
In particular, inversion seems to be of growing importance in the IMO.
Indeed, the strong candidates usually combine a mixture of various tools to solve some hard geometry problems.
And one has to figure out the tools that fit into their styles.
There are more flexibilities since there are usually numerous solutions to geometry problems.


2.5 Definitions and Facts in Plane Geometry

Unlike the other intermediate notes, this note does not tell you how to solve geometry problems.
It only contains a list of the definitions and useful (and non-useful) facts in geometry without proof.
Everyone should read the list at least once so as to be familiar with those facts.
In particular, those items which are well-known and useful are known to all best candidates in the IMO.
If you do not know them, you may need to waste a lot of time to discover such results during the contest and work out some proofs for these.
Therefore, the second step of learning geometry is to study and memorize all the facts found in some common configurations
(the first step is of course to learn the basic knowledge).
This gives us some good insight when solving geometry problems as we can easily identify some useful results and reject some impossible scenarios.
Among the items, some are useful while some are mainly for fun.
One may follow the (subjective) indicators given in the items to see which results are more commonly used.

Apart from that, one can also regard this list of facts as some additional problems of different difficulties for practice.

If you find some more useful facts which are not listed here, please let me know.


2.6 Calculating Geometry

This note is mainly about using trigonometry and length chasing to solve geometry problems.
It is useful for those who are strong in algebra.
Trigonometry is sometimes necessary, so even the strong candidates in geometry should know some standard ways of using trigonometry.
This note is not the best of this kind, but at least it shows some standard tricks and ideas related to trigonometry and length chasing.


2.7 Coordinate Geometry

Coordinate geometry is one way of solving geometry problems.
In recent IMOs, problems which can be solved by bashing are usually avoided.
So coordinate geometry may not be too useful.
However, sometimes it can still be used in the selection tests, APMO, IGO, etc.

Only those who are extremely strong in handling algebraic expressions should use coordinate geometry.
In particular, one has to be very careful, or one cannot gain any point by making any mistake.
Also, only having algebraic techniques is insufficient.
One still needs to have some basic knowledge in geometry
so as to transform the problem to the one which can be solved using coordinate geometry more easily.
Again, this is because the original formulation of the problems usually prevent one to use bashing methods directly.

Therefore, one should not rely too much on this tool.
Only use coordinate geometry in the following scenarios.
- You are sure that the problem can be solved by coordinate geometry in a reasonable time.
- This is the only problem left that you might solve in the remaining time of the contest, and you are not confident with your geometric skills.


2.8 Geometry in the Complex Plane

This is analogous to the above items, to use another algebraic method, complex numbers, to solve geometry problems.
It shares some similarities with coordinate geometry but has some different applications.
The comments are essentially the same as those given for Coordinate Geometry.


2.9 Projective Geometry

This note is about using projective geometry to solve some MO geometry problems.
In the past, I couldn't find any thorough notes about solving MO problems using projective transformations.
Most materials that I could find are about harmonic divisions only.
Therefore, I collected different materials and worked out more examples on my own, and finished this note on projective geometry.
Once I was addicted to this topic.

Projective geometry is not a necessary knowledge,
but it is good for one to learn this since some trivial results that can be proved via projective geometry
are very hard to prove using classical geometric tools.
In particular, one should learn the few basic configurations and facts related to harmonic divisions,
and learn various projective theorems including Pascal's theorem and Desargues' theorem.

Pole and polar transformation is another geometric tool, which is usually considered as a kind of pure geometric tool.
Again, one should better learn those common configurations related to this transformation
for the purpose of solving some more advanced geometry problems,
because most mature contestants should know all such things.


2.10 Inversion

Inversion is another commonly used advanced tool in solving geometry problems.
Again, this is seldom a transformation that is used in the official solution,
but there is quite a number of hard problems which can be solved using inversion,
including some problems in the IMO.
Therefore, one is strongly recommended to learn something in this topic.


3. Advanced Notes

This folder contains some materials of undergraduate level.
So they may not be too useful for the IMO, but it makes no harm to learn more.
Currently there is only one note inside the folder.


3.1 Optimization of Multivariable Functions

This note was originally contained in the intermediate note Inequalities.
But since the materials are less useful and more advanced, now they are put as a separated note and put in this folder.
It is mainly about solving inequality problems using partial derivatives and the Lagrange multipliers.
One has to understand the concepts thoroughly in order to apply these methods,
since otherwise one cannot get any point by making some conceptual mistakes.

The method of Lagrange multiplier is one of the major applications of multivariable calculus in real life,
and it can be used to solve some classical inequality problems in MO.
But it could hardly be used in solving modern inequality problems.


4. Elementary Problems

This is a collection of elementary, standard and well-known problems for beginners to practise.
They are useful because beginners usually do not know where to find suitable problems for practice
as many contest problems such as TST problems are too hard for them,
while examples in class are already solved by the trainers.
The aim of this collection is to fill up the gap.
After going through these elementary problems and reading more materials,
one is expected to be strong enough to work on the TST problems or even the IMO Shortlist.


5. Fun

As its name suggests, the materials in this folder are mainly for fun.
No need to read the things here for the purpose of building up your MO knowledge.


5.1 Expansion Methods in Inequalities

This is about solving inequality problems with expansion methods, such as Muirhead, Schur, uvw method, etc.
These things used to be useful.
Indeed, they are useful in solving classical inequality problems, but they could hardly be used nowadays.
For this reason, these materials are now put in this fun folder.
But it is still good to learn the methods introduced here.


5.2 Three-dimensinoal Geometry

3D geometry problems only appeared in the old IMOs, but not in this century.
However, it is still possible that such problems appear in other contests such as IGO and Sharygin Geometry Olympiad.


5.3 Coordinate Geometry in the IMO Shortlist

This includes the solutions to many IMO Shortlist problems using coordinate geometry.
One purpose is to justify that coordinate geometry is not as useless as one may think.
However, don't be fooled by this.
In some problems, I actually spent a rather long time (maybe a few days) to figure out a possible way to use coordinate geometry,
or even use some important observations from the geometric solutions to set up the things so as to simplify the computations.
Therefore, in practice, one probably cannot solve that many IMO level problems within a reasonable time.

6. CHKMO and IMO HK TST

These contain problems and solutions to parts of our team selection.
Other parts (Prelim and APMO) can easily be found online at some other places.
These files are put in the first folder of our IMO training instead of 有生之年系列.
However, I still regard this as part of the series.
Indeed, there was no official past paper (not even problems) for the IMO training in the past.
From time to time, there were trainees asking about this.
Therefore, finally I decided to collect all the problems and solutions and make up these files.
Most materials were already there, but it was still tedious to type everything again.

The main purposes are to keep a more official collection of the team selection test (TST) problems,
and to let the new trainees practise on these.
Let me know if you find any mistakes, better solutions and the sources of some problems (because most problems are not original).

The Hong Kong team first joined the IMO in 1988.
However, now it is almost impossible to find the TST problems in the old days.
For this reason, the collection began in the academic year 1996-97 (and 1998 for CHKMO).
The problems in the old days were not too useful anyway.
Note that year Y in IMO HK TST means the academic year Y-1 to Y, for the selection of the IMO team in year Y,
while CHKMO in year Y-1 is used for the selection of the IMO team in year Y.

Before 2004, there was no fixed format of the TST.
From 2005 to 2007, we mainly had 6 problems in test 1, 4 problems in test 2, and 4 problems in CHKMO.
In particular, test 2 was a geometry test, where all problems were geometry.
The reason was that the Hong Kong team performed badly in geometry in 2004.
So we needed some more practices.
But then in 2007, our team got very good results.
Therefore, since 2008, the geometry test switched to an ordinary test, still with 4 problems.
Since 2014, we added test 3 and test 4 which were of the same format as the real IMO.
Since 2019, we decided to use the IMO Shortlist problems in the preceding year as problems in these tests,
so as to keep the standard of the problems.
There was only test 3 but not test 4 in 2022 and 2023 because of some issues of the schedule.

If you wish to work on more contest problems for practice, you can search for many of them on AoPS.
Here are some suggestions.
- (TST level) Baltic Way, Junior Balkan MO (JBMO), Tournament of Towns (ToT), Iranian Geometry Olympiad (IGO)
- (IMO level) IMO Shortlist, Balkan MO, EGMO, ELMO, IGO
For stronger candidates, the TST of the strong countries are also worth to try.
This includes China, Hungary (Kürschák Competition), Iran, Korea, Russia (239 and All-Russian MO), USA, etc.
